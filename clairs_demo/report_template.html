Somatic variant calling · analysis

What the ClairS demo calls actually say

Twenty-nine somatic SNVs in 100 kb of chr17, called three ways from the same tumour–normal cell-line pair. The accuracy numbers are the least interesting thing in them.

01 — DETECTION

One variant separates the platforms

Three platforms, three matches to ClairS's published expected output, and not a single false positive between them.

In a tumour–normal design, precision is the number that carries the weight — a somatic false positive is an artefact the matched normal was supposed to veto. The normal did its job everywhere: NAF = 0.0000 at all 85 calls, with zero alt-supporting reads in the normal sample at every one.

Both long-read platforms miss the same site, and it is the lowest-VAF truth variant in the window.

The one miss · chr17:80,094,483 T>C

SEQC2 consensus VAF 0.096 (95 % CI 0.085–0.110), normal VAF 0.002.

ONT and PacBio never emit it — not even as LowQual. Illumina calls it PASS at AF 0.0642 (7 alt reads of 109), with 0 of 68 alt reads in the normal.

It fails the default --snv_min_af 0.05 candidate threshold on the long-read runs, so it is filtered out before the neural networks ever see it. The knob for this class of miss is the AF threshold, not the model.

02 — POPULATION STRUCTURE

This window is one clonal population, not a VAF spectrum

The truth allele fractions are very nearly a point mass.

min 0.096   Q1 0.192   median 0.197   Q3 0.203   max 0.404
                    interquartile range = 0.011

Twenty-seven of the 29 sites lie between 0.178 and 0.214 — a single clonal population at VAF ≈ 0.20. The exceptions are the 0.096 site above and one at chr17:80,062,263 (C>G, VAF 0.404), which all three platforms call.

That shape is why this window makes an easy benchmark: it is essentially 27 copies of the same easy question plus one hard one. It is also what breaks the next section.

03 — THE STATISTIC THAT LIES

Per-site AF agreement cannot be measured here

Pearson r says PacBio tracks the consensus VAF best. The rank correlation says the opposite. Both are computed from the same 28 points.

PlatformPearson rSpearman ρmedian AF ÷ SEQC2mean abs. error
ONT R10.4.10.4640.0511.0970.038
Illumina NovaSeq0.6030.3960.9570.037
PacBio Revio0.801−0.2301.0400.025

The r values are carried almost entirely by the single leverage point at VAF 0.404, sitting far from the cluster. Remove it and no relationship remains. The reason is arithmetic rather than anything about ClairS: at AF ≈ 0.2 and ~100× depth, the binomial sampling error on one site's allele fraction swamps the entire spread being measured.

No caller could rank these 27 sites correctly from ~100× data; the ordering is sampling noise by construction. What can be said is that the aggregate allele fraction is unbiased: the median ratio of ClairS AF to SEQC2 VAF is 0.96–1.10, and the mean absolute error (0.025–0.038) matches the binomial standard error almost exactly — the residual is fully explained by read sampling, leaving no room for a systematic AF bias on any platform.

Methodological note

The SEQC2 TVAF is itself a consensus over ~60 short-read replicate call sets — an orthogonal reference, not ground truth to the third decimal. That is a second reason not to over-read a correlation coefficient computed against it.

Alt reads sit near a 50/50 strand split on all three platforms. The long-read runs lean slightly reverse (median 0.39–0.44), but no individual call is one-sided enough to look like a strand artefact — consistent with the zero false positives. QUAL is low in absolute terms everywhere (6–24) because these are all ~20 % VAF somatic calls; that is the expected operating point, and the model-specific PASS threshold is calibrated for it.

05 — MUTATIONAL SIGNATURE

The spectrum is APOBEC

Twenty-seven of 29 mutations are at C:G base pairs, and the context is not random.

SubstitutionC>AC>GC>TT>AT>CT>G
truth SNVs3717011

C>T dominates with a substantial C>G shoulder. That joint elevation is the hallmark of APOBEC (COSMIC SBS2 + SBS13) rather than clock-like ageing (SBS1/5), which produces C>T without the C>G component. Trinucleotide context makes it quantitative — APOBEC3 deaminates cytosine in a TCW (TCA/TCT) context:

observed    19 / 27 C-site mutations at TCW   =  70.4 %
background   7,184 / 51,688 C·G sites in window =  13.9 %
                                        5.1× enrichment,  p = 3.7 × 10⁻¹¹

Consistent with HCC1395 being an APOBEC-high breast line.

Two honest limits

n = 29 in a single 100 kb window is far too small for signature fitting — that needs thousands of mutations genome-wide. And the truth set is itself the product of short-read consensus calling, so any platform-specific detection bias in SEQC2 propagates here. The enrichment survives both as a qualitative call; 5.1× is not a signature exposure estimate.

06 — WHY THE H FLAG DISAGREES

The tumour has lost heterozygosity across the whole window

Germline calls made by Clair3 inside ClairS, in the same 100 kb.

Platformnormal het / homtumour het / homhet retained
ONT R10.4.1142 / 813 / 1962.1 %
PacBio Revio157 / 8418 / 20511.5 %

The normal is an ordinary heterozygous genome. In the tumour, essentially every heterozygous site has gone homozygous while the homozygous count rises to match — textbook loss of heterozygosity, reproduced independently by two platforms. HCC1395 is a highly aneuploid line, so this is expected biology, not a calling failure. It also explains a downstream oddity worth knowing about.

ONT tags 24 of 28 calls with the H flag; PacBio tags 0 of 28. It is not a tagging-quality problem — PacBio haplotags more reads than ONT (97.4 % vs 86.3 %) at comparable read length (14.8 kb vs 15.9 kb median). ClairS sets H only when both haplotypes carry reads at the site (all_hp1 × all_hp2 > 0). With almost nothing left to phase against, the PacBio run put every read overlapping a call site into HP=1 and left HP=2 empty, so the precondition fails; ONT kept a small HP=1 population alongside the HP=2 bulk and passes it. Measured straight from the haplotagged BAMs, alt reads are confined to a single haplotype at 28/28 sites on both platforms — the phasing evidence is identical, only the flag's precondition differs.

Practical consequence

In LOH regions the H flag is unreliable as a confidence signal, and its absence is not evidence against a call. Illumina never sets it at all: the ilmn platform skips the Clair3 germline and phasing stages entirely — which is also why it finished in 25 s against ~100 s for the long-read runs.

Open question

A somatic VAF of ~0.20 against a background of LOH is not self-consistent with a pure, clonal, copy-neutral tumour — it implies subclonality, altered copy number, or normal admixture in this sample. Resolving which needs the copy-number and purity estimate from ClairS's --enable_verdict module, which was not run here.

07 — SCOPE

What this does and does not establish

ClairS 0.4.5 (commit a87db4a) with Clair3 1.2.0, CPU-only on 4 threads, installed from source without Docker. Truth is the SEQC2 high-confidence somatic SNV set v1.2 restricted to the SEQC2 high-confidence BED, compared with clairs.py compare_vcf --input_filter_tag PASS. Background trinucleotide frequencies come from the GRCh38_no_alt chr17 reference over the same 100 kb.

n = 29 truth SNVs in one 100 kb window. A precision of 1.000 here says nothing about genome-wide precision — published whole-genome figures for ONT R10.4.1 at 50×/25× are 86.9 % recall and 93.0 % precision at VAF ≥ 0.05. Nothing in this report should be read as a platform ranking; the three datasets differ in coverage, and one window is one window.

Analysis reproducible with clairs_demo/analyze.py and collect_run_stats.py; every figure and value regenerates from the committed VCFs.
