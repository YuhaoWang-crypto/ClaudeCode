The risk is concentrated, not diffuse. One region — residues 47–56, binding core FVAVQDITA, peak 15-mer KEREFVAVQDITASN — is presented by 7 of the 25 DR molecules and reaches 29% of the weighted US/EU population. That single peptide is what a confirmatory assay should be built around; the rest of the sequence is unremarkable for a non-human protein of this size.

On the intrinsic scale the ligand sits at 0.38 population-weighted non-self epitope units per 100 residues, against 0.13 for the Protein A Z-domain — a elevated result on the calibrated scale. Both numbers come from the same batch, the same panel and the same thresholds, which is the only reason the comparison means anything. Read on for what the batch controls say about whether the batch is reportable at all, and for the exposure arithmetic that turns this into a dose-level call.

The panel had to be designed, not inherited

The usual starting point is the HLA-DR subset of the IEDB class II reference set — 15 molecules, widely described as broadly representative. Measured against the IEDB allele-frequency tables it is not: it reaches 90.1% US-Caucasian and 85.8% European DRB1 phenotypic coverage, 84.2% on the weighted US/EU composite. A 95–98% requirement is not met by that set at any size, because the missing coverage sits in alleles it does not contain.

Growing the panel greedily against measured coverage instead reaches the target at 21 DRB1 molecules (97.27% weighted US/EU), plus the four DRB3/4/5 molecules for the second DR molecule most people express — 25 DR molecules in total.

How promiscuous is "promiscuous"?

Reporting that a peptide binds 6 of 25 DR molecules invites the question nobody usually answers: compared to what? The batch answers it by carrying three peptides whose promiscuity is an experimental fact rather than a prediction: influenza haemagglutinin HA306-318 and the tetanus toxin epitopes p2 (830–844) and p30 (947–967). Each was checked against IEDB before being used — HA306-318 has positive human DR-restricted T-cell assays on 25 distinct DR molecules, p2 on 6, p30 on 1. PADRE was a candidate and was dropped: IEDB holds no positive human DR-restricted T-cell record for it.

The ligand's dominant core is as promiscuous as the most promiscuous epitope we have evidence for. The three universal epitopes reach 2–6 of 25 DR molecules at EL %Rank < 1; FVAVQDITA reaches 6/25 — at the top of that range. It is not an outlier. It is also not innocuous: it sits exactly where a peptide known to drive CD4 responses in most donors sits.

The same table bounds the method's sensitivity. The strong-binder tier recovers only 24% of the DR molecules a universal epitope is actually presented by — HA306-318 is positive in human T-cell assays on 25 distinct DR molecules and clears %Rank < 1 on 2 of the 25 tested here. So a peptide below the tier is unflagged, not cleared, and the confirmatory assays below are scoped on the flagged peptides plus the intact ligand for exactly that reason.

Binding prediction: two heads, not one

Every 15-mer of every sequence was scored on both NetMHCIIpan heads across all 25 DR molecules. Eluted-ligand scoring alone called 266 strong binders across the batch; requiring the binding-affinity head to agree (BA %Rank < 10) leaves 218 — 18% of EL-only calls are peptides that look presented but do not measurably bind. Every downstream number uses the consensus call.

Framework hits are not risk

A VHH framework is close to human germline V. Most of its predicted DR binders have 9-mer cores that a human repertoire has already been tolerised against, and counting them makes every antibody-derived ligand score the same. Each core here is compared to every 9-mer of the human Swiss-Prot proteome: 20.9% are exact human 9-mers, 3.8% are one substitution away.

The threshold is validated rather than asserted. Re-running the identical screen on cores drawn from shuffled versions of the same sequences — a null with the same composition and no real self-similarity — gives a hit rate of 0.9% at the 8/9 cut against 24.8% for real cores, an enrichment of 27.21×. A 5-of-9 "TCR-face" screen, the obvious shortcut, was tried first and discarded: with only five positions specified it matches the human proteome by chance several times per query and flags nearly everything.

Why this filter is not optional. Human germline VH3-23 — a sequence every human is tolerised to by construction — scores a peak promiscuity of 6/25 DR molecules on this panel, the same as the test article, and an unfiltered pIRS of 0.37 against the test article's 0.38. Raw binder counts cannot tell a camelid VHH from the human framework it resembles. After the filter the germline control falls to 0.00 and the ranking becomes readable.

Where the filter is wrong, measured

The filter's rule is "self implies tolerated". That is right often enough to be worth applying and wrong often enough to be worth measuring, so the batch carries two controls that sit on the boundary.

The filter suppresses a real epitope, and this is the clearest example of it. Myelin basic protein 85–99 is a human self peptide with positive DR-restricted T-cell assays on 10 distinct DR molecules in IEDB. On this panel it is the broadest binder in the whole batch — 10/25 DR molecules at the strong-binder tier, unfiltered pIRS 1.19, higher than any ligand here. The tolerance filter takes it to 0.00. That is the filter working as designed and being wrong: self-derived epitopes exist, autoimmunity is what they are, and a sequence-identity filter cannot see the difference. It is safe for an affinity ligand because a foreign scaffold's framework similarity to human germline is the case the filter was built for — not for anything where self-reactivity is the question.

The opposite control behaves too. CLIP occupies the groove of essentially every DR molecule as the invariant chain's placeholder, yet carries no positive human DR T-cell record in IEDB. Here it reaches 7/25 DR molecules at the weak tier and 1/25 at the strong tier, and ends at pIRS 0.00 — binding strength alone is not being read as risk.

The filter is doing work, not deleting indiscriminately: the two human self controls lose 100% of their unfiltered score, while the test article loses 0%.

The measured endpoint is an antibody, not a T cell

An ADA assay detects antibodies. A B cell only class-switches with help from a CD4 T cell recognising a peptide from the same protein, so the regions worth carrying into wet-lab work first are those where a non-self DR cluster and a predicted B-cell epitope coincide.

Linear B-cell prediction (BepiPred-2.0) is the weakest model in this pipeline — most real ADA epitopes are conformational. This layer prioritises regions; it is never a standalone claim.

If the ligand can be re-engineered

Every one of the 19 substitutions was placed at each MHC-II anchor pocket (P1, P4, P6, P9) of the dominant core and the mutated 15-mer re-scored across the panel — 76 variants in all. Wild type is presented by 8 DR molecules reaching 40% of the weighted population.

The knockouts and the designable changes are not the same substitutions. 25 variants abolish predicted presentation entirely, but every one of them is chemically disruptive — the best BLOSUM62 score among them is -1, and they sit at buried framework positions where that is a real stability risk. The most conservative useful change is P1_F47M (F->M, BLOSUM62 0 — cysteine substitutions are excluded as disulfide-scrambling liabilities), which cuts presentation from 40% to 8.8% without a chemically radical substitution. That is the trade-off to take to a stability screen — not a knockout that would probably cost the fold.

What this is, and what it is not

In-silico DR screening ranks and localises risk. It does not measure it. pIRS is a relative scale readable only against the benchmarks in this batch; it is not a predicted ADA incidence, and no in-silico method available today predicts one. The specific limits that matter for how far this output can be pushed:

  • Prediction, not presentation. NetMHCIIpan scores peptide–MHC binding. It does not model antigen uptake, endosomal proteolysis, HLA-DM editing or the ligand's conformational stability in the endosome — all of which decide what is actually presented.
  • DR only. DP and DQ contribute to CD4 responses and are excluded here by the sponsor's panel specification. DQ in particular is implicated in several biologic ADA responses.
  • The tolerance filter is a screen, not JanusMatrix. It does not require the human counterpart peptide to bind the same allele, so it errs toward calling more peptides tolerised. Every flagged core is written out with the human protein it matched, so each call is checkable.
  • No aggregation, no adjuvant effect, no repeat-dose modelling. Aggregated or particulate impurity is substantially more immunogenic than the monomer, and none of that is in scope for a sequence-based method.
  • The benchmark anchor is an argument, not a measurement. Protein A Z-domain leachate has a long controlled clinical history, but "comparable predicted epitope content to Protein A" is an inference about risk, not evidence of it.

Recommended confirmatory work (all available RUO)

  1. HLA-DR competitive binding on the 3 flagged peptides against the panel's dominant molecules — directly confirms the predicted binding, days not months, lowest cost.
  2. MAPPs (MHC-associated peptide proteomics) on monocyte-derived dendritic cells from HLA-typed donors — shows what is actually processed and presented from the intact ligand, which no predictor can.
  3. Ex-vivo PBMC / CD4 T-cell proliferation across ~50 HLA-typed donors matched to this panel, using the flagged peptides plus whole ligand — the closest available surrogate for clinical ADA risk.

All three are scoped by the peptide list this pipeline produces, which is the practical point of running it: it turns "assess the ligand" into a defined, costed experiment on 3 peptides.
